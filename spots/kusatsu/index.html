Gunma · 草津温泉

Kusatsu Onsen

A steaming hot-spring field right in the middle of town.

  • Best: January, February, March, November, December
  • Onsen towns
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 2-3 hours for a day trip, or stay overnight

Why go

Kusatsu Onsen in Gunma Prefecture is counted among Japan's most famous hot-spring towns. At its heart is the Yubatake, or hot-water field, where steaming spring water flows through long wooden channels to cool before it is sent to the baths. The water is strongly acidic and carries a distinctive sulphur smell. Old-style inns, shops and bathhouses surround the square, and the scene is especially atmospheric in the evening when it is lit up.

What to do

  • Watch steam rise over the Yubatake wooden channels
  • See the traditional yumomi hot-water cooling show, with schedules at the official site
  • Walk the lantern-lit streets and try onsen manju snacks
  • Soak in a public bath or ryokan bath (strongly acidic water)

Getting there

From Tokyo, take a JR limited express toward Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi Station (about 2.5 hours), then a connecting bus (about 25 minutes). Direct highway buses from Tokyo are also available.

When to visit

Evening, when the Yubatake is lit up and the air is cool, is the most atmospheric. Winter brings steam and snow, while autumn colours are lovely too. Check bus schedules before you go.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the upper viewing level to include the full steam field and town
  • Capture the steam against dusk light, then again after lighting begins
  • Take a close-up of the wooden channels with mineral deposits

Nearby

  • Netsunoyu, home of the yumomi performance
  • Sai-no-Kawara Park, a scenic walk with open-air baths
  • Kusatsu-Shirane and the Yugama crater lake (seasonal road access)

Good to know

Hot-spring water here is very hot and strongly acidic, so follow bathhouse rules, wash first and avoid cuts or sensitive skin issues. Wear comfortable shoes for the slopes.
