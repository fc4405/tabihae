Tokyo Tower

The glowing red tower that still defines Tokyo's skyline.

  • Best: January, February, March, November, December
  • Modern & urban
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours including the observation deck and a walk around the grounds.

Why go

Opened in 1958 as a broadcasting tower, Tokyo Tower was inspired by the Eiffel Tower but stands taller, painted in white and bright orange-red. It has appeared in countless films, anime and dramas, and still feels warmly nostalgic next to the glass skyscrapers around it. Visitors can ride up to the observation decks for views over Tokyo, and the tower is lit up after dark, which makes it especially photogenic at night.

What to do

  • Ride up to the main observation deck for city views
  • See the tower lit up after dark
  • Look for Mt. Fuji on clear winter days
  • Walk to Zojo-ji for the classic temple-and-tower frame

Getting there

From central Tokyo, take the Toei Oedo Line to Akabanebashi Station, or the Hibiya Line to Kamiyacho, and walk about 5-10 minutes. Daimon and Onarimon stations are also within walking distance.

When to visit

Go around sunset to see the city turn from day to night. Winter air is clearer, and weekday evenings are calmer than weekends.

Photo tips

  • Frame the tower with the Zojo-ji gate or temple roofs for a classic old-meets-new shot
  • Shoot from Shiba Park or the street around dusk when the lights come on
  • Use a window-hugging lens hood or dark clothing to cut reflections on the deck glass

Nearby

  • Zojo-ji Temple right next to the tower
  • Shiba Park for a relaxed stroll
  • Roppongi Hills or Azabudai for more city views and shopping

Good to know

Buying tickets online in advance saves queuing time, and the lower main deck is enough for most visitors if you are short on time.

Observation decks get busy at sunset and after dark, so keep tripods folded and don't block the windows for others.
