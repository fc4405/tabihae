Arima Onsen

Soak in the reddish gold waters of an ancient onsen town above Kobe.

  • Best: January, February, November, December
  • Onsen towns
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: Half a day for a day trip (3-5 hours), or one night

Why go

Arima Onsen is counted among Japan's oldest hot springs and has been a retreat for travellers for well over a thousand years. It is known for two kinds of water: the reddish-brown Kinsen, rich in iron, and the clear Ginsen. Narrow lanes lined with wooden inns, shops and small shrines wind up the hillside. It is easy to reach from Kobe or Osaka, which makes it perfect for a day trip or a relaxed overnight stay.

What to do

  • Reddish Kinsen and clear Ginsen waters
  • Wandering the narrow old lanes
  • Local sweets such as carbonated-spring senbei
  • Small shrines and temples around town

Getting there

From Shin-Kobe Station, take the subway and Kobe Electric Railway lines to Arima-Onsen Station, usually with a transfer along the way. Direct buses from Osaka and Kobe are another option.

When to visit

Autumn and winter suit a hot spring best, with leaves turning in November. Visit on a weekday morning for quieter lanes.

Photo tips

  • Capture the red-brown water at an outdoor public bath exterior or footbath
  • Shoot the narrow lanes in soft morning light before the crowds
  • Frame wooden inn facades and lanterns in the early evening

Nearby

  • Mount Rokko and Rokko Arima Ropeway
  • Kobe Kitano and Sannomiya
  • Kobe harbour area and Meriken Park

Good to know

Public bathhouses can be busy at weekends, so bring a small towel and some cash, and check opening days before you go.

Wash thoroughly before entering the bath, keep towels out of the water, and check tattoo policies before you visit.
