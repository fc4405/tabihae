Nara · 奈良公園

Nara Park

Hundreds of friendly deer roam free among ancient temples and maple trees.

  • Best: March, April, May, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 3-4 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Nara Park is a huge open park in the old capital where over a thousand sika deer wander freely. They have long been regarded as divine messengers of nearby Kasuga Taisha shrine, which is why they are protected. Buy a pack of deer crackers and many will politely bow their heads for a treat. The park also holds Todai-ji with its Great Buddha, Kofuku-ji's five-story pagoda and Kasuga Taisha's mossy stone lanterns.

What to do

  • Feed and bow to the deer with shika-senbei crackers
  • See the Great Buddha inside Todai-ji's giant wooden hall
  • Walk the stone-lantern path to Kasuga Taisha shrine
  • Admire Kofuku-ji's five-story pagoda reflected near the pond

Getting there

From Osaka (Namba) or Kyoto, take the Kintetsu line to Kintetsu-Nara Station, from which the park is a few minutes' walk. JR Nara Station is also reachable by JR line, about 20 minutes' walk from the park.

When to visit

Go early in the morning or late afternoon for softer light and fewer tour groups, and choose a weekday if you can. Autumn foliage and spring greenery are especially pretty.

Photo tips

  • Crouch to deer eye level and shoot from low for friendly close-ups.
  • Frame deer under the red-and-green maples or beside the stone lanterns of Kasuga Taisha.
  • Try early morning on the lawns near Todai-ji for soft light and few people.

Nearby

  • Todai-ji Temple and its Great Buddha Hall
  • Kasuga Taisha shrine and its lantern-lined approach
  • Naramachi, an old merchant quarter with cafes and shops

Good to know

Deer will nibble paper, maps and bags, and may nudge or bite when you hold crackers, so feed them only the official shika-senbei and show empty hands when you are done.

Bow before you feed a deer, and never chase or grab one — they do bite.
