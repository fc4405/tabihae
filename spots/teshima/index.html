Setouchi · 豊島美術館

Teshima Art Museum

A droplet-shaped shell where water beads roll across the floor.

  • Best: April, May, June, September, October, November
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 1-1.5 hours at the museum; plan a half to full day including ferries
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Teshima Art Museum sits on a hillside above the Seto Inland Sea on Teshima island, with views toward rice terraces and the water. Architect Ryue Nishizawa and artist Rei Naito designed it together as a single concrete shell with no pillars and two oval openings to the sky. Inside, water seeps from the floor and forms droplets that slowly travel and merge, moved by wind and slope. Visitors remove their shoes and simply sit or lie on the floor, watching light, breeze and water move.

What to do

  • Sit inside the shell and watch water droplets move across the floor
  • Look up through the two oval openings to the sky and trees
  • Listen to the quiet: the space is designed to be experienced slowly
  • Take in the view of rice terraces and the sea from the approach

Getting there

From Takamatsu or Uno (Okayama) take a ferry to Ieura or Karato on Teshima (roughly 30-60 minutes depending on route), then use the island shuttle bus or a rental e-bike to reach the museum.

When to visit

Calm, clear days suit the island best, and quiet weekdays are ideal for the contemplative mood. Entry is by timed reservation, so book ahead, and check the official site for opening days and fees.

Photo tips

  • Photography inside the museum is not allowed, so capture the exterior curves and the approach path instead
  • Shoot the hillside terraces and the sea from the path just before the entrance
  • Frame the shell's pale concrete against the sky or green slopes for a clean minimalist shot

Nearby

  • Teshima Yokoo House, a colorful art-filled home
  • Les Archives du Coeur, a heartbeat archive installation
  • Teshima's rice terraces near Karato

Good to know

You will take off your shoes, so wear easy slip-on footwear and clean socks. Keep voices low and move slowly; the work depends on stillness. Allow extra time for ferry timetables, which can be sparse.
