Kyoto · 錦市場

Nishiki Market Matcha

Kyoto's kitchen: a covered alley of pickles, skewers and matcha soft-serve.

  • Best: All year
  • Photogenic cafes
  • Time: 1-1.5 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Nishiki Market is a narrow covered shopping lane about four hundred meters long in central Kyoto, nicknamed Kyoto's kitchen. It has served the city's cooks for several centuries, and today more than a hundred small shops sell pickles, tofu and yuba, dashi omelets, fresh seafood and sweets. Stalls hand out samples and skewers, and matcha soft-serve is a popular treat. It is a fun, lively place to taste Kyoto food culture in one stroll.

What to do

  • Taste matcha soft-serve and sweet sesame treats
  • Sample colorful tsukemono pickles from market stalls
  • Try fluffy dashi-rolled omelet on a stick
  • Watch fresh yuba, tofu and seafood being prepared

Getting there

It is a short walk from Karasuma Station (Hankyu line) or Kawaramachi Station (Hankyu line), and one block north of Shijo-dori. From Kyoto Station, take the Karasuma subway line to Shijo Station and walk about 5 minutes.

When to visit

Go late morning to early afternoon on a weekday, since many shops close in the late afternoon and weekends are very crowded. Check the official site for current opening hours and closing days.

Photo tips

  • Shoot down the length of the covered lane for strong perspective and hanging lanterns.
  • Take close-ups of colorful pickles, skewers and sweets on the stall counters.
  • Go early on a weekday to photograph the lane with fewer people in the frame.

Nearby

  • Nishiki Tenmangu Shrine at the east end of the market
  • Teramachi and Shinkyogoku shopping arcades
  • Pontocho alley and the Kamo River

Good to know

Many shops now ask you to eat at the stall instead of while walking, so finish your snack before moving on. Bring cash, as some stalls do not accept cards.
