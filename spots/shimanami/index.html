Setouchi · しまなみ海道

Shimanami Kaido

Cycle island to island across the sparkling Seto Inland Sea

  • Best: April, May, June, September, October
  • Scenic nature
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: A full day (6-10 hours) for the whole route, or 2-3 hours for one section

Why go

The Shimanami Kaido links Onomichi in Hiroshima Prefecture with Imabari in Ehime across six islands and a chain of bridges over the Seto Inland Sea. It is about 70 kilometres long and is famous as one of Japan's best cycling routes, with a dedicated path and a blue guide line painted on the road. You roll past citrus groves, small harbours and temples, then climb onto giant bridges for wide sea views. You can ride the whole route or just a short, scenic section.

What to do

  • Riding the blue guide line over the huge suspension bridges
  • Kurushima-Kaikyo Bridge, a trio of linked suspension bridges
  • Citrus groves and small fishing harbours on Ikuchijima and other islands
  • Sunset over the Seto Sea from the island coastal roads

Getting there

Starting from Onomichi: take the JR Sanyo Line from Okayama or Hiroshima to Onomichi Station (about 1-1.5 hours), where rental bike terminals are close to the station. From the Ehime side, take the JR Yosan Line from Matsuyama to Imabari.

When to visit

Spring and autumn offer mild weather and clear views; summer is hot and winter can be windy. Go on a weekday and start early so you can finish before dark.

Photo tips

  • Stop at the viewpoints before each big bridge to capture it with the sea behind
  • Photograph your bike on the blue guide line against the water for the signature shot
  • Aim for late afternoon when low light warms the bridges and sea

Nearby

  • Onomichi's hillside temples and slope town
  • Kosanji Temple on Ikuchijima
  • Oyamazumi Shrine on Omishima

Good to know

Reserve rental bikes in advance in busy seasons, and check where you can return them if you ride one way. Electric-assist bikes make the bridge climbs much easier. Carry water, sun protection and a light rain jacket, since shops can be far apart.
