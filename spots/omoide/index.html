Shinjuku · 思い出横丁

Omoide Yokocho

Smoke, lanterns and yakitori in a Showa-era alley by the tracks.

  • Best: All year
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1-1.5 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Omoide Yokocho, meaning Memory Lane, is a tangle of narrow alleys just west of Shinjuku Station. It grew out of the black markets of postwar Tokyo and has kept its cramped, wooden-fronted look ever since. Tiny yakitori grills, noodle counters and izakaya seat only a handful of people each, so you sit shoulder to shoulder with locals. Red lanterns and drifting charcoal smoke, with trains rumbling overhead, make it feel like stepping back decades.

What to do

  • Charcoal-grilled yakitori skewers eaten at a tiny counter
  • Rows of red lanterns glowing after dusk
  • Trains rattling past on the tracks beside the alley
  • Hopping between small bars and noodle shops for a few bites each

Getting there

It is a 1-2 minute walk from the west exit of Shinjuku Station, served by JR and several subway and private lines. Look for the alley entrance near the tracks and the large Yodobashi Camera shops.

When to visit

Go after sunset, around 6-8 pm, when the lanterns are lit and the grills are going. Weekday evenings are easier for finding a seat than Friday and Saturday nights.

Photo tips

  • Shoot down the main alley at dusk, when lanterns glow but the sky still has some blue
  • Use a wide aperture or higher ISO for the dim light, and keep your camera steady against a wall
  • Ask before photographing people at counters, and focus on lanterns, signs and skewers instead

Nearby

  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, with free observation decks
  • Golden Gai, a maze of tiny themed bars
  • Shinjuku Gyoen, a large garden that is a calm contrast

Good to know

Most places seat only 6-10 people, so be ready to wait or move on. Some charge a small seating fee, and many are cash-only, so carry cash.
