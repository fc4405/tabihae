Nachi Falls

Japan's tallest single-drop waterfall, worshipped as a living god

  • Best: March, April, May, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 2-3 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Nachi Falls drops about 133 metres in a single, thundering ribbon, the highest single-drop waterfall in Japan. It has been revered since ancient times, and the waterfall itself is the object of worship at Hirou Shrine beside Kumano Nachi Taisha. It is part of the UNESCO-listed Kumano pilgrimage landscape in the Kii Mountains. The famous view pairs the falls with the vermilion three-storey pagoda of Seiganto-ji, set among deep green cedar forest.

What to do

  • Standing at the base of the falls to feel the spray and roar
  • The classic view of the three-storey Seiganto-ji pagoda with the falls behind
  • Walking the moss-covered stone steps of the Daimonzaka approach
  • Kumano Nachi Taisha shrine with its vermilion halls

Getting there

From Osaka or Nagoya take the JR Kisei Line (Limited Express Kuroshio) to Kii-Katsuura Station (about 4 hours from Shin-Osaka), then a local bus up to the Nachi-no-Taki-mae or Nachisan stop (around 30 minutes).

When to visit

Fresh green in spring and golden light in autumn are lovely, and the falls look dramatic after rain. Weekday mornings are calmest, and the area is often misty and atmospheric early in the day.

Photo tips

  • Frame the pagoda and falls together from the Seiganto-ji grounds near the Nachi shrine
  • Use a wide lens at the base of the falls and keep a cloth for the spray
  • Try a slow shutter on a tripod-free steady surface for silky water, if allowed

Nearby

  • Seiganto-ji temple and the pagoda
  • Kumano Nachi Taisha shrine
  • Daimonzaka stone-paved pilgrimage path

Good to know

Buses between Kii-Katsuura and Nachisan run only a few times an hour, so check the timetable before you go. There are many stone steps, so wear grippy shoes. Shrine and temple grounds call for quiet, respectful behaviour.
