Hakone · 大涌谷

Owakudani, Hakone

Steaming volcanic vents, black eggs and a ropeway toward Mount Fuji.

  • Best: January, February, March, April, May, September, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Onsen towns
  • Time: 1-2 hours at Owakudani; a full day for the Hakone loop

Why go

Owakudani is a volcanic valley left by an eruption of Hakone volcano about 3,000 years ago, and its slopes still hiss with steam and sulphur. Pools of natural hot spring water are used to boil kuro-tamago, eggs whose shells turn black. Local lore says eating one adds seven years to your life. The ropeway climbs over the steaming valley, and on clear days Mount Fuji appears beyond it.

What to do

  • Steaming sulphur vents seen from a short walking trail
  • Kuro-tamago black eggs boiled in hot spring pools
  • The Hakone Ropeway gliding over the volcanic valley
  • A Mount Fuji view from the ropeway and station on clear days

Getting there

From Odawara or Shinjuku, go to Hakone-Yumoto, then take the Hakone Tozan Railway and cable car to Sounzan, and the ropeway to Owakudani. Ropeway service can be suspended by volcanic gas or wind, so check the current status first.

When to visit

Go on a clear morning for the best chance of seeing Fuji, before clouds gather. Autumn and winter air is clearer, and weekdays are calmer than weekends.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the ropeway window on the Sounzan side to catch steam plumes with Fuji beyond
  • Use the walking trail to frame the yellow sulphur and white steam against the blue sky
  • Photograph the black eggs in their basket with steam behind for a quick, iconic souvenir shot

Nearby

  • Lake Ashi, with sightseeing pirate-style boat cruises
  • Hakone Shrine and its red torii standing in the lake
  • The Hakone Open-Air Museum, a sculpture park in the mountains

Good to know

The volcanic gas has a strong smell, and people with asthma or heart conditions are advised to take care. Wear sturdy shoes and bring layers, as it is noticeably cooler and windier than Odawara.
