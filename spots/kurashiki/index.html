Okayama · 倉敷美観地区

Kurashiki Bikan District

Willows, white walls and a quiet canal frozen in old Japan.

  • Best: April, May, October, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Photogenic cafes
  • Time: 2-4 hours
Plan a November route with this spot

Why go

Kurashiki grew rich as a rice-trading town in the Edo period, and its canal was once busy with boats carrying goods to the storehouses. Today the Bikan District keeps that look, with black-and-white walled storehouses, willow trees and stone bridges along the water. Many old warehouses now house museums, cafes and craft shops, so it is a lovely place to wander slowly. It is especially pretty in the evening when the lanterns and lights reflect on the canal.

What to do

  • Strolling the willow-lined canal
  • Black-and-white walled storehouses and stone bridges
  • Museums and craft shops in old warehouses
  • Cafes in converted historic buildings

Getting there

From Okayama Station, take the JR Sanyo Line to Kurashiki Station, then walk about 15 minutes to the canal area.

When to visit

Spring and autumn are comfortable for walking, and the canal looks lovely at dusk. Weekday mornings are the quietest.

Photo tips

  • Frame the willows and white walls from a canal-side bridge
  • Shoot at dusk when lanterns reflect on the water
  • Try narrow side streets for detail shots of lattice windows and tiles

Nearby

  • Ohara Museum of Art
  • Ivy Square, a former spinning mill turned complex
  • Okayama Korakuen Garden

Good to know

Some museums and shops close on certain weekdays, so check ahead and carry a little cash for small shops.

Many houses here are private homes and shops, so keep your voice low and do not block narrow lanes with tripods.
