Kumamoto · 黒川温泉

Kurokawa Onsen

A hot-spring village tucked in the woods, made for strolling in yukata

  • Best: January, February, March, April, October, November, December
  • Onsen towns
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 2-3 hours for a day visit; an overnight stay is ideal

Why go

Kurokawa Onsen is a small hot-spring village in a river valley in Kumamoto Prefecture, in the Aso region. Its ryokan blend into the surrounding trees, and there are no flashy signs, which gives the village a calm, rustic atmosphere. Visitors can use a wooden bathing pass to visit open-air baths at different inns. It is a favorite quiet getaway, especially for those who want to see Japanese onsen culture at a slower pace.

What to do

  • Use the wooden bathing pass to try open-air baths at different ryokan
  • Stroll the stream-side lanes in yukata and geta sandals
  • Enjoy a multi-course kaiseki dinner and morning soak if you stay overnight
  • Browse small shops and cafes selling local sweets and crafts

Getting there

Direct highway buses run to Kurokawa Onsen from Fukuoka (Hakata), Kumamoto and Beppu or Yufuin, taking roughly 2-3 hours; reservations are advised. Renting a car is also popular for the Aso region.

When to visit

Autumn for the colored leaves and winter for the steaming baths in cold air are especially atmospheric. Weekdays are quieter, and day visitors are most welcome in the afternoon; check each bath for day-use hours.

Photo tips

  • Frame the cedar-lined lanes and wooden bridges, with steam rising in cold weather
  • In the evening, shoot lantern-lit ryokan entrances and soft street light
  • Ask the inn before photographing guests or inside bath areas; photos are not allowed in the baths

Nearby

  • Mt. Aso and the Kusasenri grassland, an active volcano landscape
  • Yufuin, a lakeside hot-spring town with cafes and shops
  • Kokonoe Yume Otsuribashi, one of Japan's longest pedestrian suspension bridges

Good to know

Cash is useful at small shops and some baths, and bath-pass slots are limited, so buy it early in the day. Tattoos may be restricted at some baths, so ask your inn or check the bath's rules.
