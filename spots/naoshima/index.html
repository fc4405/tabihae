Setouchi · 直島

Naoshima Art Island

An island where world-class art hides in concrete, sea and old houses.

  • Best: April, May, June, September, October, November
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: A full day (6-8 hours); stay overnight to see more
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Naoshima is a small island in the Seto Inland Sea, in Kagawa Prefecture, that has been transformed into one of the world's best-known art destinations. Museums designed by architect Tadao Ando, including Benesse House and the Chichu Art Museum, sit quietly among the hills and the shoreline. Outdoor works, most famously Yayoi Kusama's giant pumpkin sculptures, stand beside the water against a backdrop of calm sea and islands. In the old fishing village of Honmura, empty houses have been turned into small art installations you can step inside.

What to do

  • See Yayoi Kusama's pumpkin sculptures beside the sea
  • Visit the Tadao Ando-designed Benesse House and Chichu Art Museum
  • Explore the Art House Project in the old Honmura village
  • Cycle between sites with views of the Seto Inland Sea

Getting there

From Okayama, take the JR Uno Line to Uno Station, then a ferry of about 20 minutes to Naoshima; ferries also run from Takamatsu. Once on the island, use the local bus, a rental bicycle or an e-bike.

When to visit

Spring and autumn offer the most comfortable weather for cycling and walking. Many museums close on certain weekdays (often Mondays), so check the official sites before you plan.

Photo tips

  • Take the pumpkin shot from a low angle so it sits against the sea and sky
  • Visit the outdoor art early or late in the day for softer light and fewer people
  • Look for Ando's concrete geometry against the sea for architectural shots

Nearby

  • Teshima Art Museum on neighbouring Teshima island
  • Inujima, a small island with a former copper refinery turned art site
  • Takamatsu and Ritsurin Garden on the Shikoku side

Good to know

Book timed tickets in advance for popular museums like Chichu Art Museum, and check the last ferry times. Photography is often not allowed inside the galleries, so follow the signs.
