Jigokudani Snow Monkeys

Wild macaques steam in a hot spring while snow falls around them.

  • Best: January, February, March, December
  • Scenic nature
  • Onsen towns
  • Time: 2-3 hours including the walk
Plan a December route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Jigokudani, meaning 'Hell Valley', is a steep, steaming valley in the mountains of Nagano where Japanese macaques live wild. In winter, the monkeys come down to soak in a hot spring pool, with snow on their fur and clouds of steam around them. A forest trail leads you to the park, and the animals roam freely a few meters from visitors. The sight of a monkey with closed eyes in hot water has become one of Japan's most recognizable images.

What to do

  • Macaques bathing in the steaming hot spring pool
  • Baby monkeys playing and being groomed by their mothers
  • A forest trail along the Yokoyu River valley
  • Snowy mountain scenery in winter

Getting there

From Nagano Station, take the Nagano Electric Railway to Yudanaka (about 50 minutes), then a bus to the trailhead and walk roughly 30 minutes through the forest to the park.

When to visit

January to February offers snow and the famous bathing monkeys, and early morning is quietest. Check current conditions and the official site for access and fees.

Photo tips

  • Use a medium zoom lens; the monkeys come close, but a bit of reach helps you capture faces.
  • Shoot on a snowy morning when steam is thickest and the light is soft.
  • Wipe your lens often, because steam and snow fog it up quickly.

Nearby

  • Shibu Onsen, a nostalgic hot spring town with nine public baths
  • Yudanaka Onsen, the starting point for the monkey park
  • Zenkoji Temple in Nagano city

Good to know

Wear shoes with good grip, as the trail can be icy and muddy. Do not feed or touch the monkeys, and avoid staring at them or showing food.

Never touch or feed the macaques — they’re wild animals in a protected park, not props.
