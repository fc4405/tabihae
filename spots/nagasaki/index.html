Nagasaki · 長崎の夜景・グラバー園

Nagasaki Night View & Glover Garden

A harbor city lit up like a bowl of jewels, plus Meiji-era Western homes.

  • Best: February, May, October, November
  • Modern & urban
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: Half a day for Glover Garden, plus 1-2 hours in the evening for the night view
Plan a November route with this spot

Why go

Nagasaki has long been Japan's window to the world, and its hillside setting around a deep harbor makes it unusually photogenic. Glover Garden preserves Western-style residences from the Meiji era, including the home of Scottish merchant Thomas Glover, with views over the port. After dark, take the ropeway up Mt. Inasa, where the city's lights spread around the bay in a shape often described as a fan of jewels. It is regularly ranked among the most beautiful night views in Japan and beyond.

What to do

  • Tour the Western-style Meiji houses at Glover Garden
  • Look over Nagasaki harbor from the garden's upper terraces
  • Ride the ropeway up Mt. Inasa for the night view
  • Visit nearby Oura Church

Getting there

From Nagasaki Station take the tram to Ishibashi, then walk uphill to Glover Garden. For Mt. Inasa, take the ropeway from the Nagasaki side or a bus or taxi from the city center.

When to visit

Arrive at the mountain around sunset to watch the city shift from dusk to night. Clear, dry autumn and winter evenings give the sharpest view.

Photo tips

  • Arrive 30 minutes before sunset and shoot through blue hour
  • Brace on the railing or use a small tripod for night shots
  • At Glover Garden, frame the old houses with the harbor behind

Nearby

  • Oura Church
  • Dejima and Nagasaki Chinatown
  • Meganebashi spectacles bridge

Good to know

Glover Garden is on a steep slope with many steps, so wear comfortable shoes, and bring a jacket for the windy mountaintop at night.

Oura Church next door is an active place of worship, so keep your voice low and respect worshippers when visiting.
