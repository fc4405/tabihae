Akita · 角館

Kakunodate Samurai District

Walk a samurai street shaded by famous weeping cherry trees.

  • Best: April, May, October, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 2-3 hours
Plan a November route with this spot

Why go

Kakunodate is a small castle town in Akita where the samurai quarter has been preserved since the early Edo period. Dark wooden gates, black fences and old residences line a broad street, many opened to visitors as small museums. In late April the weeping cherry trees along the samurai street and the cherry-lined riverbank bloom together, drawing visitors from across Japan. In other seasons the quiet streets, autumn leaves and winter snow are just as atmospheric.

What to do

  • Weeping cherry trees on the samurai street
  • Old samurai residences open to visit
  • Riverbank cherry tree tunnel in spring
  • Quiet old town lanes in autumn and snow

Getting there

From Tokyo, ride the Akita Shinkansen to Kakunodate Station, which takes about three hours. The samurai district is roughly a 20-minute walk from the station.

When to visit

Late April for cherry blossoms, though it gets crowded then. Autumn and snowy winter days are much quieter.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the street early in the morning to get the weeping cherries without crowds
  • Use black wooden fences as a dark backdrop for pink blossoms
  • Walk the riverbank for a long tunnel of cherry trees

Nearby

  • Lake Tazawa
  • Nyuto Onsen hot spring area
  • Akita city sights

Good to know

Cherry season is very crowded and accommodation fills early, so book ahead or visit as a day trip from Morioka or Akita.

Some samurai houses are still lived in, so respect private gates and never touch or climb the cherry trees.
