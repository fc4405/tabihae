Kyoto · 宇治・平等院

Uji Matcha Town & Byodo-in

Matcha everything, plus a thousand-year-old temple on a coin.

  • Best: April, May, June, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Photogenic cafes
  • Time: 3-4 hours including the temple, lunch and tea tasting.

Why go

Uji, south of Kyoto, has been known for tea for centuries and is often called the home of Japanese matcha. Byodo-in, founded in the 11th century and a UNESCO World Heritage site, is famous for its Phoenix Hall, which reflects in the pond and appears on the 10-yen coin. Along the Uji River and the approach street, tea shops serve matcha soba, parfaits and soft serve. The Tale of Genji's final chapters are also set here, adding a literary touch.

What to do

  • See the Phoenix Hall mirrored in the pond
  • Eat matcha soba, parfaits and soft serve on the approach street
  • Walk across Uji Bridge along the Uji River
  • Visit Ujigami Shrine, a World Heritage site

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take the JR Nara Line to Uji Station in about 20-30 minutes, or the Keihan Line to Keihan Uji Station. Byodo-in is a short walk from either.

When to visit

Late April to May brings fresh tea season and green scenery, and mid-November offers autumn colours. Weekday mornings are the calmest.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the Phoenix Hall from across the pond for the classic reflection
  • Capture the green Uji River and the red-brown Uji Bridge together
  • Photograph matcha sweets near a window for soft natural light

Nearby

  • Ujigami Shrine, among the oldest surviving shrine buildings
  • The Tale of Genji Museum
  • Fushimi Inari Shrine on the way back to Kyoto

Good to know

Entry to the inside of the Phoenix Hall is by timed guided tour, so join the queue early; popular matcha shops also fill up by lunchtime.

Byodo-in is a working Buddhist temple, so keep voices low and follow staff instructions around the Phoenix Hall and grounds.
