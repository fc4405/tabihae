Kyoto · 銀閣寺・哲学の道

Ginkaku-ji & Philosopher’s Path

Zen sand gardens, then a quiet canal path under cherry and maple.

  • Best: March, April, October, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 2-3 hours including the walk
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Ginkaku-ji, officially Jisho-ji, is a Zen temple in Kyoto's Higashiyama hills, begun in 1482 as a retreat for the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa. Despite the name 'Silver Pavilion', it was never covered in silver, and its beauty comes from simple wood and moss. The raked sand garden and cone-shaped sand mound are famous. Afterward, the Philosopher's Path, a canal-side walk of about 2 km lined with cherry trees, leads gently toward Nanzen-ji.

What to do

  • The Silver Pavilion reflected in its pond
  • Ginshadan sand garden and the cone-shaped Kogetsudai
  • Moss garden and a hillside viewpoint over the temple
  • A stroll along the Philosopher's Path canal

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take a city bus bound for Ginkaku-ji (about 35 to 40 minutes) and walk up the lane from the stop. The Philosopher's Path starts near the temple entrance.

When to visit

Early April for cherry blossoms along the canal and mid to late November for maples. Go right at opening time or late afternoon to avoid crowds, and prefer weekdays.

Photo tips

  • Capture the pavilion with its pond reflection from the main path.
  • Climb the hillside trail for a high view over the sand garden and rooftops.
  • On the canal path, shoot along the water so the cherry or maple branches arch overhead.

Nearby

  • Honen-in, a quiet moss-covered temple along the Path
  • Eikando, famous for autumn leaves
  • Nanzen-ji, with its brick aqueduct and big gate

Good to know

Go early to avoid tour-group crowds, and check the official site for current hours and entry fees. Wear comfortable shoes, as the garden and the canal path both involve a fair amount of walking.
