Kyoto · 金閣寺

Kinkaku-ji

A pavilion of pure gold leaf, doubled in a mirror-still pond.

  • Best: January, February, March, April, November, December
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1-1.5 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Kinkaku-ji, officially Rokuon-ji, began in 1397 as the retirement villa of shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu and became a Zen temple after his death. The upper two floors of the pavilion are covered in gold leaf, and a golden phoenix perches on the roof. The original was burned down by a young monk in 1950 and rebuilt in 1955. Standing beside the Mirror Pond, it doubles itself in the water, which is why it is one of the most photographed sights in Kyoto.

What to do

  • The three-storey gold pavilion, each floor in a different architectural style
  • The golden phoenix ornament on the roof
  • The garden path looping around Kyoko-chi pond
  • Sekka-tei teahouse area and matcha with a view near the exit

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take a city bus bound for Kinkaku-ji (about 40 minutes), or ride the subway to Kitaoji Station and continue by bus or taxi. The bus is usually the simplest option.

When to visit

Arrive right at opening or in the late afternoon, when the light warms the gold and crowds thin. Weekdays are calmer, and winter snow or autumn foliage are especially beautiful.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the first viewpoint across the pond with the pavilion centred, and wait for the water to settle for a clean reflection
  • Use a polarizing filter or step slightly left or right to cut glare off the gold
  • Look back from the path behind the pavilion for a quieter angle with pine trees framing the building

Nearby

  • Ryoan-ji, home of the famous rock garden
  • Ninna-ji, a World Heritage temple with late-blooming cherry trees
  • Kitano Tenmangu shrine, known for plum blossoms and its flea market days

Good to know

The path is one-way and the pavilion itself cannot be entered, so there is no need to linger long. Your admission ticket is a paper talisman, a nice souvenir to keep.

The viewing platform gets crowded fast — take your photo and step back so others can get theirs too.
