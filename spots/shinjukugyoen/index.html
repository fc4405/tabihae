Shinjuku Gyoen

A calm green escape just steps from Shinjuku's neon towers.

  • Best: March, April, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 1.5-2.5 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Shinjuku Gyoen was once the residence of a feudal lord's family and later became an imperial garden before opening to the public as a national garden. It blends a Japanese landscape garden, an English-style lawn and a French formal garden in one large park. In spring the cherry trees draw crowds, and in autumn the leaves and chrysanthemums take over. The skyline of Shinjuku rising behind the trees makes for a very Tokyo photo.

What to do

  • Cherry blossom lawns in spring
  • Japanese garden with ponds and a teahouse
  • Tropical greenhouse with orchids and palms
  • French formal garden with rose beds and plane trees

Getting there

From Shinjuku Station it is a short walk to the Shinjuku Gate, or take the Marunouchi Line to Shinjuku-gyoenmae Station, which is right beside another gate.

When to visit

Late March to early April for cherry blossoms and November for autumn colour. Go on a weekday morning to avoid the biggest crowds.

Photo tips

  • Frame the Shinjuku skyscrapers over the lawn and trees for a city-meets-nature shot
  • Use the wooden footbridge and pond edges for reflections
  • Shoot inside the greenhouse on grey days when outdoor light is flat

Nearby

  • Shinjuku Station area and Omoide Yokocho alleys
  • Kabukicho neon district
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation decks

Good to know

The garden has an entrance fee and its own rules, so bring a little cash or a card and check the closing day before you go.

Alcohol and loud music are not allowed in the garden, so keep picnics calm and take all your rubbish with you.
