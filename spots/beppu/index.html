Beppu Hells (Jigoku Meguri)

Steaming ponds in cobalt blue and blood red, for looking, not bathing.

  • Best: All year
  • Onsen towns
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 2.5-4 hours for the main hells; add time for food and footbaths
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Beppu is one of Japan's great hot-spring towns, and its jigoku, or hells, are natural springs too hot to bathe in. Each pond is distinctive: the Sea Hell is cobalt blue, the Blood Pond Hell is red from iron-rich clay, and the Oniishibozu Hell bubbles with grey mud. Most are clustered in the Kannawa area, where steam rises between the buildings, and the sulfur smell fills the air. You walk around them on a short route, with a few free footbaths along the way.

What to do

  • See the cobalt-blue Sea Hell (Umi Jigoku) and its steaming pond
  • Look at the red Blood Pond Hell (Chinoike Jigoku)
  • Watch bubbling grey mud at Oniishibozu Hell
  • Try steam-cooked food and a free footbath in the Kannawa area

Getting there

From Beppu Station take a local bus toward Kannawa (about 25-30 minutes) for most of the hells. From Hakata (Fukuoka) the JR Limited Express Sonic reaches Beppu in about 2 hours.

When to visit

Cooler days and mornings make the steam clouds thicker and the temperature more comfortable. Clear, sunny days make the blue and red ponds most vivid. Check the official site for current hours and ticket details.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the blue Sea Hell on a sunny day, standing at the side of the pond to catch both steam and color
  • Include the steam plumes against dark trees or buildings for stronger contrast
  • Keep your lens or phone clean, since steam fogs lenses quickly

Nearby

  • Takegawara Onsen, a historic wooden bathhouse in central Beppu
  • Beppu Ropeway up Mt. Tsurumi for valley and bay views
  • Myoban Onsen, famous for its thatched yunohana drying huts

Good to know

The ponds are dangerously hot, so stay behind the barriers and never touch the water. Bring a small towel for the footbaths, and expect a strong sulfur smell that can cling to clothes.
