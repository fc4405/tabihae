Osaka · 道頓堀

Dotonbori

Giant crabs, runners and neon reflected in Osaka's food canal.

  • Best: All year
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours with snacking
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Dotonbori runs along a canal in the Namba district, a street that has been Osaka's entertainment centre since the early 1600s. Today it is lined with oversized signs, including the Glico Running Man, a giant mechanical crab and a pufferfish lantern. Osaka is nicknamed the nation's kitchen, and the street is a place to try takoyaki, okonomiyaki and kushikatsu. After dark the neon reflects on the water, which makes it one of Japan's most photographed scenes.

What to do

  • Pose under the Glico Running Man sign from Ebisu Bridge
  • Spot the giant moving crab and pufferfish signs
  • Eat takoyaki, okonomiyaki and kushikatsu street-side
  • Take a short canal cruise for a view from the water

Getting there

Walk about five minutes from Namba Station, served by the Midosuji, Yotsubashi and Sennichimae subway lines plus Nankai and Kintetsu trains. From Shin-Osaka, take the Midosuji Line directly to Namba.

When to visit

Go around sunset and stay into the evening for the neon at blue hour. Weekdays are calmer, while weekend nights get very crowded around the bridge.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the Glico sign from Ebisu Bridge, with the crowd as foreground
  • Walk to the Tombori River Walk for a low view of signs reflected in the canal
  • Use blue hour so the sky still has colour behind the neon

Nearby

  • Hozenji Yokocho, a stone-paved lantern-lit alley
  • Kuromon Market, known as Osaka's kitchen
  • Shinsaibashi-suji arcade and Amerikamura for shopping

Good to know

Watch your step and your bag in the dense crowd, and eat street food in the designated spot or in front of the stall rather than while walking.
