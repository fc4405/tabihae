Ise Jingu

Japan's most sacred shrine, hidden in an ancient cedar forest by a clear river.

  • Best: All year
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 3-4 hours for both shrines
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Ise Jingu is the most sacred shrine in Shinto and is dedicated to the sun goddess Amaterasu. It is actually a complex of many shrines, centered on the Naiku (Inner Shrine) and the Geku (Outer Shrine). Its main halls are rebuilt every 20 years in plain, unpainted wood in an ancient style, a tradition that has continued for over a thousand years. Walking the gravel paths under towering trees feels calm and timeless.

What to do

  • Cross the wooden Uji Bridge into the Naiku grounds
  • Wash your hands in the clear Isuzu River at the riverside
  • Walk the gravel path among giant cedars to the main sanctuary
  • Visit the Geku Outer Shrine, traditionally visited first

Getting there

From Osaka Namba, Kyoto or Nagoya, take the Kintetsu line to Iseshi Station (Geku is a short walk) or Ujiyamada Station. For Naiku, take a local bus from Iseshi or Ujiyamada, about 15-20 minutes.

When to visit

Go in the morning when the air is cool and crowds are small, ideally on a weekday. The forest is atmospheric in every season, and New Year brings very large crowds.

Photo tips

  • Shoot Uji Bridge and the big torii from the riverbank steps in soft morning light.
  • Photograph the towering cedars and gravel path from low for a sense of scale.
  • Photography is not allowed in front of the main sanctuary halls, so take photos of the paths and bridge instead.

Nearby

  • Oharai-machi and Okage Yokocho, old-style streets near Naiku
  • Meoto Iwa, the wedded rocks at Futami
  • Toba for pearls and a bay cruise

Good to know

This is an active place of worship: bow at the torii, walk to the side of the path, and dress modestly. Wear comfortable shoes for the long gravel walk.

Photography is banned at the innermost sanctuary (Naiku’s core building) — respect the roped-off boundary even if others don’t.
