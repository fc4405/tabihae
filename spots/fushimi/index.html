Kyoto · 伏見稲荷大社

Fushimi Inari Taisha

A tunnel of vermilion gates winding up a sacred mountain

  • Best: January, February, March, April, October, November, December
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours for the Senbon Torii area; 2.5-3 hours for the full summit loop
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Fushimi Inari Taisha is the head shrine of the many Inari shrines found across Japan, dedicated to the deity of rice, business and prosperity. Its famous trails are lined with thousands of torii gates, each donated by a company or individual, which form glowing orange corridors as they climb Mt. Inari. Fox statues, the messengers of Inari, watch over the paths. The shrine grounds are open all day, so you can wander as far up the mountain as you like.

What to do

  • Walk the Senbon Torii, the dense twin lanes of gates just beyond the inner shrine
  • Spot the fox statues holding keys or jewels in their mouths
  • Hike the loop to the summit of Mt. Inari for quiet forest and city views
  • Try the street food and souvenir stalls along the approach road

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take the JR Nara Line to Inari Station, about 5 minutes; the shrine entrance is directly across from the station. Keihan Line's Fushimi-Inari Station is also a short walk away.

When to visit

Go early in the morning or in the evening to avoid the heaviest crowds, and weekdays are calmer than weekends. The gates look striking in every season, and the paths are lit softly after dark.

Photo tips

  • Shoot at dawn or late in the evening to get gates with few people in frame
  • Stand low at the start of the Senbon Torii and use a long lens to compress the rows of gates
  • Look for the inscriptions on the back of the gates and the small shrines higher up for detail shots

Nearby

  • Tofuku-ji Temple, known for its autumn leaves and a bridge over a valley
  • Sanjusangen-do Hall with its 1,001 statues of Kannon
  • Fushimi sake district, with sake breweries and canal-side streets

Good to know

Wear comfortable shoes with good grip, because the mountain trail has many steps and the crowd thins out the higher you go. Bring a drink and keep to the path, as the upper trail has few shops.

Shoot quickly at the gates — worshippers use this path daily. Don’t block the torii tunnel for a photo.
