Setouchi · 犬島精錬所美術館

Inujima Seirensho Art Museum

A ruined copper refinery reborn as an art museum, powered by the earth.

  • Best: April, May, June, September, October, November
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 2-3 hours including the ferry timing
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Inujima is a small island in the Seto Inland Sea, and its Seirensho Art Museum is built inside the remains of a copper refinery that operated for only about ten years in the early 1900s. The architect Hiroshi Sambuichi wove the brick ruins, chimney and slag-brick walls into a new building that uses natural airflow and geothermal energy instead of conventional air conditioning. Inside, the work of artist Yukinori Yanagi, inspired by the writer Yukio Mishima, fills the dim passages. Of the Setouchi art islands, it feels among the quietest and most atmospheric.

What to do

  • The brick chimney and refinery ruins visible from the island
  • Architecture that uses natural ventilation and earth heat
  • Yukinori Yanagi's installation inside the dim museum passages
  • The Inujima Art House Project works scattered through the village

Getting there

From Okayama Station, travel by bus or taxi to Hoden Port, then take the short ferry (about 10 minutes) to Inujima. Ferry times are limited, so check the timetable before you go.

When to visit

Choose a calm, clear day, ideally on a weekday when ferries are less crowded. Spring and autumn are comfortable for walking around the small island.

Photo tips

  • Photograph the brick chimney and ruins from outside against the sky, as indoor photography is generally restricted
  • Look for texture shots of slag bricks and weathered walls along the outdoor path
  • Capture the island village and small art houses in soft evening light

Nearby

  • Inujima Art House Project, small installations in the village
  • Inujima Life Garden, a greenhouse garden near the museum
  • Okayama Korakuen, one of Japan's celebrated landscape gardens on the mainland

Good to know

There are few shops and the ferries are infrequent, so plan your return sailing and bring water and snacks. Museum opening days and ticketing change, so check the official site before you go.
