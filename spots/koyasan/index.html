Wakayama · 高野山奥之院

Okunoin, Koyasan

Walk two quiet kilometres under ancient cedars to a saint's tomb.

  • Best: March, April, May, September, October, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours for the path, half a day with other sights
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Okunoin is the most sacred site of Koyasan, the mountaintop centre of Shingon Buddhism founded by Kobo Daishi (Kukai) in the early 9th century. A path of about two kilometres runs from the entrance bridge through towering cedar forest lined with moss-covered gravestones, memorials and Jizo statues. At the far end stands the mausoleum of Kobo Daishi, who is believed to rest in eternal meditation, beside the lantern hall Torodo filled with countless glowing lights. It feels solemn, quiet and unlike anywhere else in Japan.

What to do

  • Walk the mossy cedar-lined path from Ichinohashi Bridge
  • See the lantern hall Torodo glowing with countless lights
  • Spot Jizo statues and ancient gravestones of feudal lords
  • Pay respects at Kobo Daishi's mausoleum (no photos beyond the bridge)

Getting there

From Osaka Namba Station take the Nankai Koya Line to Gokurakubashi (about 1.5 hours), ride the cable car up, then a bus to Okunoin-mae. You can also walk the path from the Ichinohashi entrance.

When to visit

Early morning or late afternoon gives the quietest, most atmospheric light, and evening lantern-lit walks are popular. Autumn colour and snow add beauty, but winter is cold, so dress warmly.

Photo tips

  • Shoot down the main path with sunlight filtering through the cedars
  • Close-ups of mossy stone pagodas and Jizo statues with red bibs
  • Use a tripod-free higher ISO for the dim forest, and respect photo-restricted areas

Nearby

  • Danjo Garan, the temple complex with the vermilion Konpon Daito pagoda
  • Kongobuji, the head temple of Shingon Buddhism with famed rock garden
  • Reihokan Museum, with Buddhist art and treasures

Good to know

This is a working cemetery and sacred site, so stay quiet, dress modestly, bow at the bridge and do not photograph beyond Gobyo-bashi. A temple stay is a memorable way to extend your visit.

This is an active pilgrimage site, not a photo backdrop — keep voices low along the path and don’t step onto the grave plots.
