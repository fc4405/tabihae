Tokushima · 祖谷のかずら橋

Iya Valley Vine Bridge

Cross a bridge made of vines, high above a hidden Shikoku gorge.

  • Best: May, June, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1-2 hours at the bridge; a half day to a full day with the wider valley.
Plan a November route with this spot

Why go

Deep in the mountains of Tokushima Prefecture on Shikoku, the Iya Valley has long been one of Japan's most remote regions. Its Kazurabashi bridge is woven from thick wild vines and spans the Iya River far below, and it is rebuilt periodically using traditional techniques. Legend links such bridges to fugitives who could cut them down behind them. Crossing the gaps between the planks is a thrill, and the steep forested gorge makes a dramatic backdrop in every season.

What to do

  • Walk across the vine bridge over the Iya River
  • See the nearby Okuiya double vine bridges
  • Look for the famous peeing boy statue on the cliff road
  • Soak in the Iya Onsen riverside bath

Getting there

From Osaka, take a train to JR Awa-Ikeda or Oboke Station, then a local bus or taxi into the valley. Renting a car or joining a tour from Osaka or Takamatsu is often easiest.

When to visit

Late October to November brings autumn colour, and May to June is lush green. Visit on weekday mornings to avoid the crowds.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the riverside path below to show the bridge against the gorge
  • Take photos from the bank before crossing, since you will need both hands on the ropes
  • Use a wide lens to capture the bridge, river and forested cliffs together

Nearby

  • Oboke Gorge sightseeing boat
  • Iya Onsen riverside open-air bath
  • Okuiya Double Vine Bridges deeper in the valley

Good to know

Wear flat, secure shoes and bring some cash, as roads are winding and the area has few shops and limited public transport, so check return times.

The bridge is narrow and gaps show the river below, so cross slowly, hold the ropes, and don't stop to pose in the middle.
