Yamagata · 銀山温泉

Ginzan Onsen

A river lined with Taisho-era wooden inns, glowing in the snow.

  • Best: January, February, December
  • Onsen towns
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1-2 hours as a day trip; stay overnight for the full atmosphere

Why go

Ginzan Onsen is a small hot-spring village in the mountains of Yamagata, once linked to a silver mine that gave it its name. Its three- and four-story wooden ryokan, built mostly in the Taisho period, face each other across a narrow river with arched footbridges. After dark the gas-style lamps come on, and in winter the snow makes the scene look like an old woodblock print. The village is compact and can be walked in under an hour.

What to do

  • Walk along the Ginzan River past the row of wooden ryokan
  • Cross Shirogane Bridge and other small arched bridges for classic views
  • Visit a public bath or soak in a ryokan day-use bath where available
  • See the old mine tunnel area at the upper end of the village

Getting there

From Tokyo ride the Yamagata Shinkansen to Oishida Station (about 3 hours), then take a bus to Ginzan Onsen (about 40 minutes). Buses also run from Obanazawa Station; check the timetable as services are limited.

When to visit

Winter evenings, December to March, are the most famous time, but snow also makes roads and buses less predictable. Autumn color and fresh green are quieter alternatives, and weekdays are calmer than weekends.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from Shirogane Bridge looking along the river at dusk, when the lamps are on but the sky still has blue
  • Brace your elbows and use night mode to keep the warm window lights soft
  • Include the river and a footbridge in the foreground to add depth to the ryokan facades

Nearby

  • Obanazawa town and its local soba noodles
  • Yamadera, the mountain temple, as another Yamagata stop
  • Oishida Station area for the Shinkansen connection

Good to know

Day visitors cannot drive into the village center, so use the designated parking and walk or shuttle in. Please keep quiet and do not photograph into guest rooms or fly drones, as people are staying and living here.
