Tokyo · 秋葉原

Akihabara

Neon-bright towers of anime, games and gadgets, all in one district.

  • Best: All year
  • Anime & pop culture
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 2-4 hours (more if you love games and collectibles)
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Akihabara in Tokyo began as an electronics district after the war and gradually became the centre of Japan's anime, manga and game culture. Multi-storey shops stack figures, trading cards, retro games and secondhand goods floor after floor, while giant advertisements cover the building fronts. Arcades with claw machines and rhythm games stay busy, and themed cafes, including maid cafes, add to the unique atmosphere. After dark, the signs light up and the whole district looks like a scene from an anime.

What to do

  • Browse multi-floor shops for figures, cards and retro games
  • Play claw machines and rhythm games in the big arcades
  • Try a themed or maid cafe for a one-of-a-kind experience
  • Walk Chuo-dori main street when the neon signs light up

Getting there

Akihabara Station is on the JR Yamanote and Keihin-Tohoku lines, the JR Sobu Line, the Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line and the Tsukuba Express. It is about 5-10 minutes by JR from Tokyo Station.

When to visit

Weekends are liveliest but very crowded; weekday afternoons are easier for browsing. Visit around dusk to see the shops by day and the neon at night.

Photo tips

  • Shoot Chuo-dori from a crosswalk at dusk to catch the neon and the crowd
  • Use the brightly lit signs as background for night portraits
  • Capture colourful shelves and claw machines, but check each shop's photo rules first

Nearby

  • Kanda Myojin Shrine, a short walk away, popular with anime fans
  • Ueno Park and the Ameyoko market, a couple of stops by train
  • Jimbocho, Tokyo's famous second-hand bookshop district

Good to know

Carry your passport for tax-free purchases at large stores, and keep some cash for small shops and arcade machines. Please do not photograph staff or people on the street without asking.
