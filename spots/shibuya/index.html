Shibuya Scramble

Every light change releases a human tidal wave in all directions.

  • Best: All year
  • Modern & urban
  • Time: 30 minutes for the crossing, 1.5-2 hours with Shibuya Sky
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Shibuya Crossing sits right outside Shibuya Station's Hachiko Exit. When the signals turn, traffic stops in every direction and hundreds of people cross at once, even diagonally. Surrounded by giant video screens and neon billboards, it has become a symbol of modern Tokyo and a famous film location. It looks completely different from street level, from a cafe window, or from the open-air deck of Shibuya Sky high above.

What to do

  • Cross with the crowd, including the diagonal shortcut
  • See the whole scene from the Shibuya Sky open-air deck
  • Say hello to the Hachiko statue near the station
  • Wander the shops and food of nearby Center Gai

Getting there

Get off at Shibuya Station, which is served by JR Yamanote Line and several Tokyo Metro and private lines, and take the Hachiko Exit. The crossing is directly in front of you.

When to visit

Dusk into night is the most dramatic, when the screens and signs glow. Weekday daytime is easier to move around; weekend evenings are the busiest.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from a high window or the Shibuya Sky deck for the classic top-down view
  • At street level, wait for the light to change and use a slightly slower shutter to blur the crowd
  • Try a low-angle shot from the Hachiko side with the screens behind you

Nearby

  • Meiji Jingu and Yoyogi Park, a calm forest shrine nearby
  • Omotesando and Harajuku for fashion and street food
  • Nonbei Yokocho, tiny retro bars close to the station

Good to know

Shibuya Sky uses timed tickets, so book ahead for sunset slots, and keep to the sidewalks when taking photos because the crossing is a real, busy road.
