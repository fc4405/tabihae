Yamaguchi · 元乃隅神社

Motonosumi Inari Shrine

A winding tunnel of red torii hugging the coast of the Sea of Japan.

  • Best: April, May, June, September, October
  • Scenic nature
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 45 minutes to 1 hour on site
Plan a April route with this spot

Why go

Motonosumi Inari Shrine sits on the coast of Nagato in Yamaguchi Prefecture, facing the Sea of Japan. A long line of vermillion torii gates winds down from the hillside toward the cliff edge, with the ocean opening up beside them. The shrine is relatively young compared with many famous ones, yet it became widely known through photographs of the red gates against deep blue water. It is a remote spot, and that sense of quiet isolation is part of its appeal.

What to do

  • Walk down the long line of red torii gates
  • Try tossing a coin into the offering box atop the big gate
  • Look out over the cliffs and sea from the viewing points
  • Photograph the gates against the ocean

Getting there

The easiest way is by car or taxi from Nagato. Public buses and trains run infrequently, so check schedules and plan your return before you go.

When to visit

Clear days make the red gates stand out against the sea, and softer light in the morning or late afternoon is most flattering. Avoid strong wind and storm days.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the top of the gate line looking down toward the sea
  • Wait for gaps in the crowd for a clean tunnel shot
  • Use a wide angle to include the cliff and coastline

Nearby

  • Nagato and Yumoto Onsen hot spring area
  • Senjojiki coastal cliffs
  • Hagi castle town

Good to know

Wear sturdy, non-slip shoes, as the paths near the gates can be uneven and slippery after rain. Allow extra time because this area is far from major hubs.

This is an active shrine, so bow before the gates, keep to the path and take care near the cliff edge, especially when it is windy.
