Naha, Okinawa · 首里城

Shuri Castle

The vermilion palace of the Ryukyu Kingdom above Naha

  • Best: All year
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours; add 1 hour for nearby sights
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Shuri Castle was the royal palace of the Ryukyu Kingdom, which flourished for centuries through trade between Japan, China and Southeast Asia. Its vermilion halls and curved stone walls blend Chinese and Japanese styles with a distinct Okinawan character, and the site is part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing. The main hall was destroyed by fire in 2019 and is being rebuilt, but the gates, walls and grounds are still well worth visiting. Check the official site for what is currently open.

What to do

  • Pass through Shureimon Gate, the iconic symbol of Okinawa
  • Walk the curved stone ramparts and castle gates for views over Naha
  • See the main hall reconstruction area and the exhibits explaining the kingdom's history
  • Visit the nearby royal mausoleum Tamaudun and stone-paved streets

Getting there

From Naha Airport or central Naha, ride the Yui Rail monorail to Shuri Station (about 25-30 minutes from the airport); the castle is a 15-minute walk uphill. Gibo Station is another option.

When to visit

Morning light is gentle and crowds are thinner; weekdays are calmer. Winter to spring brings cooler walking weather, while summer can be very hot, so bring water and a hat.

Photo tips

  • Frame Shureimon Gate with its stacked tiled roofs against the blue sky
  • Shoot along the curved stone walls with a wide lens to show their flowing lines
  • From the upper terraces, capture Naha's rooftops and the sea in the distance

Nearby

  • Tamaudun, the royal mausoleum of the Ryukyu kings
  • Shuri Kinjo-cho stone-paved road, an atmospheric old street
  • Kokusai-dori in central Naha for souvenirs and Okinawan food

Good to know

The main hall is under reconstruction, so expect scaffolding or viewing areas in place of the finished building; check the official site for current access, hours and fees before you go. Wear comfortable shoes because the grounds are hilly.
