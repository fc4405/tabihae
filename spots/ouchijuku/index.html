Ouchi-juku Thatched Post Town

Step back into Edo-period Japan on a street lined with thatched roofs.

  • Best: January, February, June, October, November
  • Retro Japan
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours
Plan a November route with this spot

Why go

Ouchi-juku is a former post town in the Aizu region of Fukushima, on a road that once connected Aizu with Nikko and Edo. A single wide street runs between rows of thatched-roof houses, many now serving as soba restaurants, craft shops and snack stalls. It is known for its negi soba, eaten with a whole green onion used as a chopstick. Walking up the street, then climbing to the viewpoint at its end, gives the best sense of this carefully preserved village in the mountains.

What to do

  • Walk the main street lined with thatched-roof houses
  • Try negi soba eaten with a green onion
  • Climb to the viewpoint for a view over the village
  • Browse local crafts and snack stalls

Getting there

Reach Aizu-Wakamatsu from Tokyo by train, then continue by local bus or taxi to Ouchi-juku. Bus services are limited, so check schedules in advance.

When to visit

Winter snow turns the village into a postcard scene, while autumn colours and fresh greenery are also lovely. Arrive in the morning, before tour groups gather.

Photo tips

  • Shoot straight down the street from the viewpoint at the top
  • Frame a thatched roof close up for texture and detail
  • Include snow or autumn leaves for a seasonal mood

Nearby

  • Tsuruga Castle in Aizu-Wakamatsu
  • Higashiyama Onsen hot spring area
  • Aizu-Wakamatsu old town streets

Good to know

Wear warm clothing and non-slip shoes in winter, as the street can be snowy and icy. Carry some cash, since many small shops and stalls prefer it.

Locals still live and run shops here, so stay off private land, ask before photographing people and do not walk on the thatched roofs or walls.
