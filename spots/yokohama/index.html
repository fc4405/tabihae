Yokohama · 横浜赤レンガ倉庫

Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse

Old brick harbour warehouses with a modern skyline behind them

  • Best: All year
  • Modern & urban
  • Photogenic cafes
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours
Plan a November route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

The Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse is a pair of brick customs warehouses built in the early 1900s on the waterfront. After years of decline they were restored and reopened in 2002 as a cultural and shopping complex, with one building for exhibitions and events and the other for shops and restaurants. The harbourside setting with the Minato Mirai skyline and the Cosmo Clock ferris wheel makes it especially lovely at sunset. Seasonal events such as the winter Christmas market and spring-to-autumn festivals fill the plaza.

What to do

  • The restored brick buildings and their arched windows
  • Shops and restaurants inside Warehouse No. 2
  • The open harbourside plaza with seasonal markets and events
  • Skyline views with the Cosmo Clock ferris wheel

Getting there

From Yokohama Station take the Minatomirai Line to Bashamichi Station (about 10 minutes), then walk about 6 minutes. Alternatively walk 15-20 minutes from JR Sakuragicho Station or Minatomirai Station, passing the harbour area.

When to visit

Late afternoon into evening is best, when the warm light turns to illuminated skyline views. Weekday visits are calmer, while winter and festival weekends are lively but crowded; check the official site for event dates.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the brick facade from the plaza at golden hour with the harbour behind it
  • Frame the Cosmo Clock ferris wheel and skyline with the warehouse in the foreground
  • Capture the illuminated buildings after dusk, from the waterfront or the Osanbashi pier

Nearby

  • Cosmo World and the Cosmo Clock 21 ferris wheel
  • Yamashita Park and the waterfront promenade
  • Yokohama Chinatown

Good to know

Harbour winds can make evenings much colder than they feel in the city, so bring a light jacket. The grounds are free to walk around, but check the official site for events or market entry rules. Shops and cafes may be busy at weekends, so book restaurants ahead.
