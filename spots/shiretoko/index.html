Shiretoko Peninsula

Hokkaido's wild edge: mirror lakes, bears and a hot waterfall.

  • Best: June, July, August, September
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: Full day minimum; 2 days to see the highlights
Plan a June route with this spotTours & tickets nearby (Klook)

Why go

Shiretoko is a remote peninsula in eastern Hokkaido, listed as a UNESCO Natural World Heritage Site for its rich ecosystem where sea and land connect. The Shiretoko Five Lakes reflect the mountains like mirrors, and Kamuiwakka Hot Falls is a river warmed by volcanic heat. Brown bears and other wildlife live here, and in winter drift ice drifts in from the Sea of Okhotsk. It is one of Japan's last true wilderness areas and a rewarding challenge to reach.

What to do

  • Walk the Shiretoko Five Lakes boardwalk for mountain reflections
  • See the warm waters of Kamuiwakka Hot Falls
  • Join a wildlife or boat cruise from Utoro or Rausu
  • See Oronko Rock and sunset views over the Sea of Okhotsk

Getting there

Fly to Memanbetsu Airport and take a bus to Utoro, or reach Shiretoko-Shari Station by JR and then take a bus. Allow most of a day for the journey from Sapporo.

When to visit

Summer and early autumn are best for hiking and cruises, while February offers drift ice season. Some roads and facilities close in winter.

Photo tips

  • Come on a calm, early morning for mirror reflections on the lakes
  • Use a polarizing filter to cut glare on the water
  • Keep a zoom lens ready for wildlife, and shoot from a safe distance

Nearby

  • Oshinkoshin Falls near Utoro
  • Shiretoko Pass viewpoint toward the Rausu side
  • Utoro harbor and its sunset spots

Good to know

Check the park's current bear and access rules before you go, as some trails need a guide or registration, and stock up on supplies in town.

Brown bears live here: keep your distance, never feed wildlife, and follow all park rules and guided-access requirements.
