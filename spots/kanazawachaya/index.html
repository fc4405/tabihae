Kanazawa · ひがし茶屋街

Higashi Chaya District, Kanazawa

Stone-paved lanes and lattice teahouses straight out of old Japan.

  • Best: January, February, March, April, November, December
  • Retro Japan
  • Photogenic cafes
  • Time: 1-1.5 hours, or 2 hours with a cafe stop.

Why go

Higashi Chaya is one of Kanazawa's historic geisha districts, laid out in the early 19th century during the Kaga domain era. Its wooden teahouses with fine lattice fronts line a stone-paved main street and are among the best-preserved in Japan. Today some still host geisha performances, while others have become cafes, craft shops and sweet shops, many using Kanazawa's famous gold leaf. The calm, golden light of early morning and the lantern glow at dusk make it a very photogenic stroll.

What to do

  • Stroll the stone-paved main street of lattice teahouses
  • Try gold-leaf soft serve or sweets
  • Visit a preserved teahouse open to the public
  • Browse craft shops for gold-leaf and local crafts

Getting there

From JR Kanazawa Station, take a local loop bus for about 15 minutes to the Hashibacho stop and walk a few minutes. Walking from the station takes around 25-30 minutes.

When to visit

Visit early in the morning or at dusk to avoid daytime crowds. Spring blossoms and winter snow both add lovely atmosphere.

Photo tips

  • Shoot straight down the main street at first light for an empty, symmetrical lane
  • Focus on lattice windows and paper lanterns for close-up detail shots
  • Come at dusk when the lanterns glow against the dark wood

Nearby

  • Omicho Market for seafood and local snacks
  • Kenroku-en, one of Japan's most celebrated gardens
  • Kazuemachi and the Asano River teahouse streets

Good to know

Cafes and sweet shops fill up around midday, so go early and carry some cash for small shops and snacks.

Many teahouses are private and still in use, so don't enter closed doors, and ask before photographing any geisha or residents.
