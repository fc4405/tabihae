Hiroshima · 厳島神社

Itsukushima Torii

A giant vermilion gate that seems to float on the sea

  • Best: January, February, March, April, September, October, November
  • Scenic nature
  • Retro Japan
  • Time: Half a day, 3-4 hours; add more if you hike or ride the ropeway

Why go

Itsukushima Shrine sits on Miyajima, an island in the Seto Inland Sea near Hiroshima. Its buildings stand on stilts over the water, and the great torii gate stands out in the bay, so at high tide the shrine and gate seem to float. The shrine took its present form in the 12th century under the warrior Taira no Kiyomori, and it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At low tide you can walk across the sand to stand right beneath the gate.

What to do

  • See the Great Torii from the shrine's corridors at high tide
  • Walk out to the gate across the sand at low tide
  • Ride the ropeway or hike Mt. Misen for views over the Seto Inland Sea
  • Meet the friendly wild deer and try momiji manju maple-leaf cakes on Omotesando street

Getting there

From Hiroshima, take the JR Sanyo Line to Miyajimaguchi Station (about 30 minutes), then a short JR or Matsudai ferry to Miyajima (about 10 minutes). The shrine is a 10-15 minute walk from the pier.

When to visit

Check the tide table before you go: high tide gives the 'floating' look, low tide lets you walk to the gate. Visit on a weekday morning or stay until evening, when the gate is often lit after dark.

Photo tips

  • For the classic 'floating' shot, shoot from the shrine's corridor or the shoreline at high tide
  • At low tide, stand near the gate base to show its scale against people on the sand
  • Sunset and the evening light-up give the gate a glowing reflection on calm water

Nearby

  • Mt. Misen ropeway and trails with views of the Seto Inland Sea
  • Daisho-in Temple, a peaceful temple on the island's slope
  • Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park and Museum, easy to add on the way back

Good to know

Do not feed the deer and keep snacks out of sight, as they will grab paper and food. Shrine entry has a small fee and a one-way route, so check the official site for current fees and any restoration work.

The floating torii stands on sacred shrine ground — don’t climb on the base, even at low tide.
