Tottori · 鳥取砂丘

Tottori Sand Dunes

A desert-like dune landscape meeting the Sea of Japan.

  • Best: May, June, September, October
  • Scenic nature
  • Time: 2-3 hours
Plan a May route with this spot

Why go

The Tottori Sand Dunes are the largest dunes in Japan, stretching along the Sea of Japan coast in a protected natural area. Shaped over a very long time by sand carried by rivers and wind, they create rippling patterns and sweeping ridges. You can climb the big dune for sea views, ride a camel, or try sandboarding and paragliding. The scenery feels more like a desert than Japan, which makes it wonderfully photogenic.

What to do

  • Climb the big dune for views of the Sea of Japan
  • See the wind-rippled sand patterns
  • Try a camel ride or sandboarding
  • Visit the nearby sand sculpture museum

Getting there

From Tottori Station, take a local bus toward the dunes, about 20 minutes. Tottori is reached by limited express train from Okayama or Kyoto/Osaka.

When to visit

Late afternoon and sunset give long shadows and golden light; spring and autumn avoid the heat. Clear days after a windy night often show the best sand ripples.

Photo tips

  • Shoot the ridge lines at golden hour so the shadows show off the curves
  • Include small figures walking up the dune to show the huge scale
  • Capture fresh wind ripples before many footprints cover the sand

Nearby

  • The Sand Museum with large sand sculptures
  • Uradome Coast with its scenic rocky islets
  • Tottori city's local seafood

Good to know

Wear shoes that can handle sand (or go barefoot in the cool season), and bring sunglasses and water. The sand can get very hot in summer and the wind can be strong.

The dunes are a protected natural area, so take your litter home, stay out of fenced vegetation, and do not dig or remove sand.
